## Figure 1: Predictive Performance Across Multilingual Settings

- **Filenames**: `fig01_predictive_performance.png`, `fig01_predictive_performance.pdf`
- **Purpose**: Compare overall predictive classification performance across the three code-mixed language settings.
- **Source Artifact**: [`results/final/master_experimental_results.csv`](file:///c:/Users/SKSOH/OneDrive/Desktop/Team6_XAI/results/final/master_experimental_results.csv)
- **Metrics Represented**:
  - Overall Accuracy: HECM (0.815), Tamil (0.710), Malayalam (0.919)
  - Macro-averaged F1: HECM (0.770), Tamil (0.469), Malayalam (0.450)
  - Weighted-averaged F1: HECM (0.810), Tamil (0.731), Malayalam (0.908)
- **Interpretation**:
  - Highlights the critical disparity between overall Accuracy and class-balanced Macro-F1.
  - In Malayalam and Tamil, high overall Accuracy (up to 91.9%) is deceptive due to severe class imbalance (88.35% majority non-offensive samples in Malayalam). Macro-F1 reveals that minority offensive categories suffer substantial performance degradation (Macro-F1 drops to 0.450).
- **Important Limitation / Caveat**:
  - High accuracy alone does not imply reliable or robust multilingual hate speech detection.

## Figure 2: Confidence Calibration Error & Overconfidence Audit

- **Filenames**: `fig02_calibration_ece.png`, `fig02_calibration_ece.pdf`
- **Purpose**: Evaluate the trustworthiness and probabilistic calibration of model posterior probabilities.
- **Source Artifacts**:

  - Mean Posterior Confidence: HECM (0.882), Tamil (0.777), Malayalam (0.984)

## Figure 6: SHAP Deletion-Based Behavioral Relevance

- **Filenames**: `fig06_shap_faithfulness.png`, `fig06_shap_faithfulness.pdf`
- **Purpose**: Measure whether removing top tokens identified by KernelSHAP causes statistically significant decision degradation.
- **Source Artifact**: [`results/final/master_experimental_results.csv`](file:///c:/Users/SKSOH/OneDrive/Desktop/Team6_XAI/results/final/master_experimental_results.csv)
- **Metrics Represented**:
  - Mean Confidence Drop on Top Token Deletion: HECM (0.0215), Tamil (0.0376), Malayalam (0.0658)
  - Median Confidence Drop: HECM (0.0071), Tamil (0.0036), Malayalam (0.0001)
  - Prediction-Change / Flip Rate: HECM (13.0%), Tamil (18.0%), Malayalam (8.0%)
- **Interpretation**:
  - Deleting top SHAP tokens consistently causes statistically significant confidence drops compared to random or bottom tokens ($p < 0.001$ across all three languages).
  - Decision flips occur in 8% to 18% of audited samples upon deleting just a single top token.
- **Important Limitation / Caveat**:
  - Captures **behavioral relevance under deletion**, NOT causal faithfulness or ground-truth explanation correctness. Token deletion introduces out-of-distribution syntax artifacts.

## Figure 7: Statistical Audit of Self-Attention Behavioral Faithfulness

- **Filenames**: `fig07_attention_faithfulness.png`, `fig07_attention_faithfulness.pdf`
- **Purpose**: Statistically test whether final-layer self-attention weights reflect faithful behavioral attribution under token deletion.
- **Source Artifact**: [`results/final/master_experimental_results.csv`](file:///c:/Users/SKSOH/OneDrive/Desktop/Team6_XAI/results/final/master_experimental_results.csv)
- **Metrics Represented**:
  - One-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test $p$-values visualized as $-\log_{10}(p)$
  - Significance threshold at $\alpha = 0.05$ ($-\log_{10}(0.05) \approx 1.301$)
  - Top vs. Random Deletion: HECM ($p = 0.157$, $-\log_{10}(p) = 0.80$), Tamil ($p = 0.621$, $-\log_{10}(p) = 0.21$), Malayalam ($p = 0.080$, $-\log_{10}(p) = 1.10$)
  - Top vs. Bottom Deletion: HECM ($p = 0.012$), Tamil ($p = 0.944$), Malayalam ($p = 0.038$)
- **Interpretation**:
  - **Self-attention fails behavioral faithfulness across all three language settings**: In no language does Top-vs-Random token deletion achieve statistical significance ($p > 0.05$).
  - In Tamil, even Top vs. Bottom attention deletion fails completely ($p = 0.944$).
- **Important Limitation / Caveat**:
  - Attention is an internal routing mechanism, not an explanation. Lack of behavioral faithfulness does not mean attention weights are useless for representation learning.

## Figure 8: Cross-Method Explainability Agreement (Attention vs. SHAP)

- **Filenames**: `fig08_attention_shap_agreement.png`, `fig08_attention_shap_agreement.pdf`
- **Purpose**: Quantify attribution alignment between Self-Attention and KernelSHAP.
- **Source Artifact**: [`results/final/master_experimental_results.csv`](file:///c:/Users/SKSOH/OneDrive/Desktop/Team6_XAI/results/final/master_experimental_results.csv)
- **Metrics Represented**:
  - Exact Top-1 Word Agreement: HECM (23.0%), Tamil (18.0%), Malayalam (26.0%)
  - Mean Spearman Rank Correlation ($\bar{\rho}$): HECM (0.313), Tamil (0.186), Malayalam (0.168)
  - Median Spearman Rank Correlation ($\tilde{\rho}$): HECM (0.332), Tamil (0.211), Malayalam (0.244)
- **Interpretation**:
  - Agreement between Self-Attention and SHAP is weak-to-moderate. Top token agreement never exceeds 26%.
  - Rank correlations are positive but modest ($\rho \in [0.17, 0.33]$), demonstrating that attention and SHAP prioritize divergent token subsets.
- **Important Limitation / Caveat**:
  - Agreement between two methods does not establish ground-truth correctness; both methods could share systematic biases.

## Figure 10: Performance Degradation Across Script Composition Subsets

- **Filenames**: `fig10_script_composition.png`, `fig10_script_composition.pdf`
- **Purpose**: Evaluate performance disparities on tokens exhibiting mixed-script Unicode representations.
- **Source Artifacts**:
  - [`results/robustness/script_composition_per_class_metrics.csv`](file:///c:/Users/SKSOH/OneDrive/Desktop/Team6_XAI/results/robustness/script_composition_per_class_metrics.csv)
  - [`results/final/master_experimental_results.csv`](file:///c:/Users/SKSOH/OneDrive/Desktop/Team6_XAI/results/final/master_experimental_results.csv)
- **Metrics Represented**:
  - Single-script vs. Mixed-script Macro-F1:
    - HECM: 0.772 vs. 0.675 (Drop: $-12.6\%$)
    - Tamil: 0.468 vs. 0.452 (Drop: $-3.3\%$)
    - Malayalam: 0.446 vs. 0.336 (Drop: $-24.8\%$)
  - Evaluated sample support: HECM ($N=1,322$ vs $76$), Tamil ($N=6,311$ vs $273$), Malayalam ($N=2,584$ vs $413$)
- **Interpretation**:
  - Significant Macro-F1 degradation occurs when text contains co-occurring Latin and Native Indic characters (up to $-24.8\%$ relative drop in Malayalam).
- **Important Limitation / Caveat**:
  - Script composition is purely Unicode block-based. Transliterated regional text written entirely in Latin script is classified as Single-script (Latin). Thus, script composition is an imperfect proxy for true linguistic code-mixing.
